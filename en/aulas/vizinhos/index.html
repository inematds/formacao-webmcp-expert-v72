Lesson 5 · 14 min

Neighbor protocols as a map: A2A, ACP, AP2, NLWeb, llms.txt — what is still a draft

Every week a new acronym shows up promising to make your site "AI-ready." Almost all of them are drafts or proposals, and each one lives in a different layer. In this lesson you place each neighbor protocol on the map, with a status and a date, and decide for your site: adopt, wait, or does not apply.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Which layer each acronym lives in
  2. 2Proposal, draft, project, and stable, with a date
  3. 3Adopt, wait, or does not apply, with a reason
  4. 4The reasons that don't count (ranking, "it will become a standard")

Goal: place each neighbor protocol in the right layer with a dated status, and decide adopt, wait, or does not apply, with a reason.

02 · How to read the status

The label tells you how much you can bet on it.

1. Proposal

Someone published the format; nobody has committed to reading it.

E.g., llms.txt. It costs little, but don't promise any effect.

2. Draft

A group document (CG, IETF) still in progress. The API has already changed once (navigator → document).

Adopting = review date + fallback, always.

3. Project

It has an implementation and an organization behind it (Linux Foundation, a company).

It is not stable: check the version on the day you adopt it.

4. Stable

A published, versioned revision, with a announced change (MCP 2026-07-28).

Even so, pin the SDK version and run the battery when it goes up.

Script ruleAdopting something that is not stable requires revisar_em (a date) and fallback.

"It will be the standard of the web" and "it moves the site up in the AI rankings" are not reasons: nothing on the map says that. Adopting WebMCP without a review date and without a fallback also fails.
